DPSS: A Great Place to Work is a UX design project completed through Atlas Digital Consulting Group, where our team partnered with the University of Michigan’s Division of Public Safety and Security to design a careers-focused microsite. The goal was to clearly communicate DPSS’s values, highlight career opportunities beyond law enforcement, and create a more approachable, community-centered recruitment experience.

This was a design-only project, with a team composed entirely of UX designers and no developers.

## The problem {/* toc: Problem */}

<TwoColumn leftTitle="The current site struggles with" rightTitle="This project aimed to">
<div slot="left">

- Careers experience focused mainly on job listings, not storytelling
- DPSS’s mission and values were not clearly communicated
- Non-law-enforcement roles were underrepresented
- External applicants faced confusion and friction when applying

</div>
<div slot="right">

- Clearly communicate DPSS’s mission and community impact
- Highlight diverse roles across the organization
- Create a welcoming, accessible careers experience
- Reduce friction in job exploration and application
- Support both desktop and mobile users

</div>
</TwoColumn>

## Discovery & research {/* toc: Research */}

### Understanding the landscape

From initial client meetings, we identified key constraints and opportunities:

- **Accessibility Priority:** PDF brochures needed web alternatives
- **Technical Context:** Design for Sanity CMS implementation
- **Brand Alignment:** Follow UMich brand guidelines with DPSS nuances
- **Audience Diversity:** Serve everyone from external law enforcement candidates to current students

### Competitive analysis

Our team reviewed six career sites (Hilton, Marriott, Coca-Cola, Synchrony, Michigan State Police and Ohio State Patrol) to identify common patterns. The competitive analysis revealed a clear gap: public safety career sites tend to emphasize functionality over emotional connection, while hospitality sites excel at human storytelling but often lack institutional credibility.

## Defining the approach {/* toc: Approach */}

### Key insights & design principles

From research, we established three guiding principles:

<NumberedList>

1. **Humanize Public Safety** Move beyond enforcement imagery to show community engagement and wellness support (including their therapy dog!)
2. **Reduce Cognitive Load** Simplify navigation and minimize clicks, especially for job applications
3. **Balance Professionalism & Approachability** Maintain institutional credibility while feeling welcoming

</NumberedList>

Together, these principles address weaknesses in competitor sites while amplifying their strengths.

### Project scope & constraints

- Design within existing DPSS website architecture (not standalone)
- High-fidelity prototypes for 3 core pages: Landing, Job Listings, Detailed Job Description
- Timeline: 14 weeks with December launch target

### User flow

After establishing our information architecture, we mapped the primary user journey for candidates. This user flow ensured our designs would guide visitors from initial interest to application submission with minimal friction:

Key flow decisions:

- **Progressive Engagement:** 'Why Join DPSS' → 'Explore Opportunities' → 'Apply'
- **Reduced Cognitive Load:** Visual step-by-step application graphic
- **Flexible Pathways:** Multiple application options (portal + HR contact)
- **Ongoing Connection:** Newsletter signup to nurture longer-term candidates

### Visual design direction

<Figure
  src={moodboard}
  alt="Mood board collage: officers walking and laughing together, a supportive handshake, an event security officer, a ‘Mental Health’ illustration on maize yellow, the Michigan M logo for Public Safety & Security on navy, and a sunlit tree on campus."
/>

We started with a mood board to solve a core challenge: public safety recruitment materials often feel institutional and intimidating. Our visual direction needed to humanize DPSS while maintaining professional credibility.

Our moodboard emphasized:

- **Color:** UMich blue with complementary earth tones
- **Imagery:** Authentic photos of DPSS staff in community settings
- **Tone:** Professional yet approachable, avoiding corporate coldness

The mood board helped us:

- Visualize 'approachable professionalism' before designing a single screen
- Align stakeholders on emotional tone early in the process
- Establish visual guardrails for our design system
- Bridge the gap between UMich's brand guidelines and DPSS's unique community-focused identity

This foundation ensured our final designs didn't just function well; they felt right for the audience and organization.

### Design system

To ensure a professional and on-brand experience, we established a custom design system using DPSS's official assets. This system provided a unified visual language and reusable building blocks to accelerate prototyping and maintain consistency across the entire microsite.

<Figure
  src={designSystem}
  alt="DPSS design system sheet: eight brand color swatches (maize, navy, blue, teal, grey, tan, near-black and white), primary, secondary and tertiary buttons, the DPSS logo, a type scale from Title to Note, and component samples including an Apply Now button and a Job Summary accordion."
/>

Key Deliverables:

- **Brand Foundation:** Formally defined the color palette, typography scale, and logo usage.
- **Interactive Components:** Built reusable UI elements like hover-aware buttons and interactive accordions to enable clear, engaging user interactions.
- **Style Guide:** Documented styles for headings, body text, and interactive states to streamline high-fidelity design.

### Wireframing

Our low-fidelity wireframes focused on solving specific pain points before adding visual polish. This phase was about information architecture, content hierarchy, and user flow, the invisible structure that makes or breaks the user experience.

The goal for the home page was to make DPSS’s community-centered brand feel immediate and accessible, not overwhelming. To achieve this, the team focused on establishing a clear information hierarchy in the lo-fi wireframes, prioritizing scannability and user control. The layout was built on the principle of progressive disclosure.

**Homepage wireframe decisions**

- **High-Level Summary First:** Presents only the core value proposition and key information points upfront.
- **Strategic Use of Dropdowns:** Tucks detailed content behind interactive elements, allowing users to "pull" information as they need.
- **Visual & Textual Balance:** Uses spacing and clear groupings to prevent dense "walls of text."
- **User-Controlled Depth:** Empowers readers to choose their own path, scan the overview, or dive into specifics.

#### Job Listings + Job Details

To improve the job application experience, our team identified several key issues on the existing pages:

- **Repetitive and text-heavy content** across the job description and application.
- **A broken external applicant flow** that incorrectly required UMich authentication.
- **A disjointed user journey,** where the application opened confusingly in a third-party pop-up.

#### Key design decisions

<NumberedList>

1. **Clear Content Hierarchy** Organized information with a "benefits first" approach to help candidates understand DPSS's culture before seeing job listings.

   <TagList
     label="Design principles"
     tags={['Progressive Disclosure', 'Information Architecture']}
   />

2. **Visual Job Discovery System** Transformed dense job listings into scannable cards with essential filters and save/share functionality.

   <TagList label="Design principles" tags={['Cognitive Load Reduction', 'User Control']} />

3. **Streamlined Application Flow** Redesigned job pages to highlight critical details with a persistent "Apply Now" button for straightforward application.

   <TagList label="Design principles" tags={['Clear Pathways', 'Action-Oriented Design']} />

</NumberedList>

## Results & response {/* toc: Impact */}

The redesign successfully transformed the recruitment experience into a more welcoming, community-focused platform. The DPSS team appreciated how the design balanced institutional credibility with approachable warmth, noting the deliverables were ready for their upcoming hiring campaign.

### My top 3 contributions

<NumberedList>

1. **Led stakeholder collaboration across departments.** Facilitated clear communication between design team and DPSS.
2. **Managed parallel design workflow.** Led a team of 5, coordinating 4 UX designers across a 14-week timeline.
3. **Prepared implementation-ready handoff.** Ensured designs met brand standards and technical requirements.

</NumberedList>
