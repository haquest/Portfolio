The **Michigan Healthy Nail Salon Cooperative (MHNSC)** is a group of University of Michigan researchers and students working to protect the health and safety of nail salon workers and owners through three pillars: **research, education, and advocacy**. Many of the workers it serves are immigrants, often Vietnamese, with **limited English proficiency**.

Its website was meant to be their hub for safety resources and free chemical safety training. Our year-long UMSI capstone set out to make it one.

<Callout variant="note" label="The constraint">
  The client team maintains the site on their own, so **everything had to be built directly in
  Google Sites**: no custom code and no new platform. The prototype _is_ the live website.
</Callout>

## Why couldn’t salon workers find safety information on a site built for them? {/* toc: Challenge */}

Nail salon workers face ongoing exposure to harmful chemicals and occupational hazards, yet access to clear, usable health and safety information remains limited. MHNSC’s website was **functional but overly academic and text-heavy**, and it was not built for the people who needed it most.

<TwoColumn leftTitle="The original site" leftIcon="alert" rightTitle="Our goals" rightIcon="target">
<div slot="left">

- **Safety information scattered** across Resources, Salon Safety and About
- **Dense, academic text** and jargon like “The Toxic Trio”
- Infographics with **text locked inside images**: no alt text, impossible to translate
- **No direct way to contact the director**
- Inconsistent, dated visuals that **undermined trust**

</div>
<div slot="right">

- Reorganize navigation around **how users think**
- Simplify content to **plain language** (6th–8th grade)
- Make **contact pathways** explicit and easy to scan
- Prioritize **scannable, visual** formats
- Keep it **maintainable in Google Sites** after handoff

</div>
</TwoColumn>

<Callout variant="problem">
  Users lack a reliable and intuitive way to locate, understand, and engage with critical health and
  safety resources on the MHNSC website.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="hmw">
  How might we make MHNSC’s health and safety resources easy to find, understand, and trust, for
  workers with varying levels of English, within the limits of Google Sites?
</Callout>

## What we learned from users {/* toc: Research */}

We used a sequential, mixed-methods approach. Each method built on the last: **card sorting** explored how people group information, **usability testing** showed how they actually move through the site, and a **survey** checked the patterns we saw.

Participants were young adults (21–25) with **direct ties to the nail salon industry**: family-run salons, a former salon receptionist, relatives who are licensed technicians. We recruited them through the Vietnamese Student Association, other student groups, family and friends. Because the group was small and specific, **we report exact fractions instead of percentages**.

### Three key insights

<Finding n="F1" kicker="Insight ONE" title="Navigation didn’t match how users think">

Users completed **4 of 5 tasks on average, but by trial and error**, often checking the wrong section first, like looking in Resources before finding safety tips in Salon Safety. In the card sort, both participants put the core safety cards into one group of their own: they expected a single **“safety hub.”** Toggle between the two participants’ sorts below.

<Finding n="F2" kicker="Insight TWO" title="Jargon and dense text blocked understanding">

Specialized labels caused hesitation and confusion, and long paragraphs were especially hard for **someone less proficient in English**. Every participant asked for information to be **“chunked up”** and visual.

<TagList
  label="Labels participants couldn’t place"
  tags={['The Toxic Trio', 'Routes of Exposure', 'Improving Ergonomics']}
/>

<PullQuote by="Round 1 participant">
  I hate, like, reading, like, a lot of words, or, like, big paragraphs… chunk it up, I guess, but
  also be succinct and, like, straightforward.
</PullQuote>

</Finding>

<Finding n="F3" kicker="Insight THREE" title="No clear contact path undermined trust">

**Every participant failed to find the director’s contact information.** They fell back on a general form, unsure their message would reach the right person. In the survey, the two participants who struggled most rated their likelihood to recommend the site **1/5 and 2/5**.

### A new information architecture

Card sorting became our new site map. **Safety content now lives in one Salon Safety hub** with three clear categories, “What We Do” became the three **pillars** on About, and **In the News moved under Resources**, which now separates MHNSC’s own work from external resources.

<Callout variant="note" label="Reading the numbers honestly">
  With three participants, the confidence gain wasn’t statistically significant (paired t-test, p >
  .05), and one participant’s confidence dipped. We treated it as a **directional signal** and
  leaned on what we observed: fewer wrong turns, clearer paths to contact information and a
  unanimous preference for the redesign.
</Callout>

- **Clearer organization**: tabs felt “more directive”

## What worked. What I learned. {/* toc: Reflection */}

<TwoColumn leftTitle="What worked" leftIcon="sparkle" rightTitle="What I learned" rightIcon="lightbulb">
<div slot="left">

- **Anchoring every decision in research**, from principles to wireframe critiques
- **Testing the real build** instead of mockups, round after round
- **Designing for handoff**, so the client can own the site after we leave

</div>
<div slot="right">

- **Constraints shape good solutions**: a translation banner instead of a language toggle we couldn’t build
- **Plain language is accessibility**: clearer English helps everyone, especially multilingual users
- **Small samples need honest reporting**: fractions and directional signals, not big claims

</div>
