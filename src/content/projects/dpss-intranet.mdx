## Why did a tool used by 553 people stop working for nearly all of them? {/* toc: Challenge */}

The University of Michigan's Division of Public Safety and Security was transitioning away from Confluence. The existing site—"ComHub"—was plagued by usability issues staff worked around rather than relied upon.

<Callout variant="problem">
  DPSS staff across all roles and campuses cannot reliably access, find, or trust the tools and
  information they need—resulting in workarounds, disengagement, and operational risk.
</Callout>

## Three key findings {/* toc: Findings */}

<Finding n="F1" kicker="Finding ONE" title="Fragmented Ecosystem, No Single Source of Truth">

Critical systems operated in isolation. BRIEFS had been offline since the July 2024 CrowdStrike incident. Telestaff required duplicate data entry. Cross-unit IT systems failed to integrate—leaving staff to triangulate between disconnected tools with no hub.

<PullQuote by="Research participant">
  Every time I make them sign in or click redundantly, it's taking time from delivering service.
</PullQuote>

**Documented gaps**

- BRIEFS offline 18+ months
- Duplicate data entry required
- No cross-unit IT integration
- Zero unified access point

</Finding>

<Finding n="F2" kicker="Finding TWO" title="Failed Trust and Content Governance">

No content governance existed at any level. Org charts were approximately one year out of date. Over 75% of roster-listed staff were no longer employed by DPSS. Policy documents were up to ten years old. No channel framework meant no mechanism to flag, archive, or retire stale content.

<PullQuote by="Research participant">
  Less is more. Accuracy is the big thing. Just being able to know if I can rely on it.
</PullQuote>

<Finding n="F3" kicker="Finding THREE" title="Structural Barriers to Access">

Long-scroll layout, broken search, and no role-based organization made the intranet structurally hostile to everyday use. Poor mobile experience compounded the problem for field staff. Most critically: Dearborn and Flint campuses were entirely absent from the platform.

<PullQuote by="Research participant">
  The fact that they haven't received much energy or attention has been noticeable.
</PullQuote>

## A self-reinforcing cycle of failure {/* toc: Feedback Loop */}

Every breakdown fed the next. The key leverage point was not search, not content—it was **Trust**.

## Six strategic priorities {/* toc: Recommendations */}

<NumberedList>

1. **Role-Based Information Architecture** Organize navigation around who you are, not where content happens to live in a folder tree.
2. **Integrate PowerDMS** Centralize policy management with a single auditable, authoritative source used across all campuses.
3. **Build Dearborn and Flint Presence** Regional campus inclusion as a first-class design requirement—not an afterthought or phase two.
4. **Content Governance Model** Assign owners, establish review cycles, implement archive flags and retirement protocols throughout.
5. **Prioritize Search** Functional, role-aware search as a platform-level requirement—not an optional feature to layer on later.
6. **Set as Default Homepage** Drive adoption through habit formation. Make the intranet the ambient workspace, not an afterthought.

</NumberedList>

### Key takeaway

<PullQuote by="Executive Cabinet, DPSS">
  If you build it the right way, it really becomes such a great training and onboarding system for
  them. It's guiding them rather than the human capital we're investing in for consistency.
</PullQuote>

The intranet was used by nearly everyone—but wasn't working for nearly anyone. The opportunity: build a single, trustworthy, role-sensitive environment that reduces friction, supports emergency response, connects regional campuses, and gives leadership a reliable communications channel.
